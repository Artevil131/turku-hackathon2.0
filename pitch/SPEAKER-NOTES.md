## 2. Imagine (1:00)

**A real person at a big decision, and nobody tells them who pays.**

Optional opener: "Quick show of hands: who here has bought a used car, or a flat?"

"Imagine you're 18. You've saved up for your first car, and you've found it: a 2009 BMW for €6,900. It
looks great. But what should you ask the seller? What will it really cost? And if something goes wrong,
who pays?

Almost half of these BMWs failed their last inspection. If the gearbox goes next month, insurance doesn't
pay. You do. And it's a private seller, so it's your problem.

Or you're buying your first flat: two rooms in a block from 1974, for €159,000. If the pipe renovation
starts next year, your share is about €51,600, and that isn't insurance either.

Today, you find out after you've signed. We want to move that moment to before you sign."

## 4. Business model (1:45)

**The free check brings people in, what they get with If keeps them, and If earns from better
customers.**

"So how does If make money from a free tool? Not from fees. From better customers.

(The loop.) Anyone can do the check, for free. It reaches people at the exact moment they need insurance:
you can't drive that BMW home without traffic insurance. One tap, and the If quote is already filled in,
so winning that customer costs almost nothing.

(The customer gets.) After signing, If customers get the reasons to stay: the reminders, a logbook,
huoltokirja, for the car or the home, with every inspection, repair and receipt in one place, and a
guarantee behind every who-pays label: if the label said covered, If stands behind it.

(If gets.) At the next life event, a rental, a home, a family, they come back, check again and add a
policy.

(The formula.) A customer is worth the years they stay, times premiums minus claims minus costs. What if…
moves all four: they stay longer, hold more policies, claim less because they chose the safer car or flat
and got warned in time, and cost less to win.

And it brings the customers who bring If the most money: first-time flat buyers first, the biggest policy
and the start of a long relationship; then households heading for several policies; and car-loan buyers,
whose lenders usually require kasko.

One catch we thought about: the most worried buyers are the keenest to insure. So the price follows the
facts the buyer confirms, like the building year or the car model, and the tool steers people to the
safer choice. Using the tool never changes a price."

## 5. Why it works (1:00)

**It's real, and people can trust it.**

"It's live today, on any phone, with nothing to install. Every number has a source: Traficom,
Isännöintiliitto, Finanssiala, Finnish law and If's own terms. Anything we estimate says so.

Our rules read the big Finnish ad sites. When they can't, our own AI model reads the ad, and every fact
has to quote it: 161 of 162 right, none invented.

It's private: only the ad is sent, screenshots only after a check, and nothing is stored. The details go
to If when the person asks for a quote.

And it's honest about who pays, even when the answer isn't insurance. That's why people will trust it."

## 6. How people find it (0:45)

**They find it where they already are, and every path ends at an If quote.**

"The cheapest path first: If's own customers, in the If app, when they look at their next car or home.
Then people who search before they buy, 'is a 2009 BMW reliable?': pages built from the same data lead
straight into a check. Then the places where people sign: landlords, housing managers, car dealers and
estate agents. Marketplaces last, paid per policy.

Every path ends in the same place: an If quote, already filled in."

## If a judge asks

- **What happens to the data?** "Only the ad's text is sent, without phone numbers or email addresses, and
  screenshots only after a check. There's no account and no tracking, nothing is stored, and the server
  logs only errors. The details go to If when the person asks for a quote. A legal review is on the
  launch list."
- **Won't people check for free and buy cheaper insurance elsewhere?** "Some will, and a check costs
  almost nothing. If wins the moment: first in line, already filled in, honest, and with a guarantee that
  only comes with an If policy."
- **Won't it attract only the risky buyers?** "The price follows the same facts the buyer confirms:
  building year, pipe renovation, car model. The tool also steers people to the safer choice. Using the
  tool never changes a price."
- **Can the AI make things up?** "Every field it returns must quote the ad, or it's dropped: 161 of 162
  right, none invented. Costs and who pays never come from the AI."
- **Do the reminders really cut claims?** "They go after the biggest claim types: leaks and winter
  driving. How much they cut is something If can measure with its own claims data."
